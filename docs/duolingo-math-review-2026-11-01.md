# BrainPet puzzle improvements informed by Duolingo Math

Review dated 1 November 2026, against repository commit `74647b47`.

Implementation update, 1 November 2026: the first revision is implemented in [the content generator](../scripts/puzzles/progression-revisions.mjs). Early addition challenges now use taught operations and small totals. Easy subtraction has ten sequenced lessons, including all application and review steps. Numeric true/false claims are balanced by topic, estimation choices use nearby multiples of ten with varied answer ranks, and fraction tasks vary scaling, simplifying, and denominator relationships. Daily introductory reviews stay within the new limits, and fraction equivalence reviews also vary their scale factors. Both English and Latvian content have been regenerated.

All 1,800 puzzle IDs, lesson IDs, skill IDs, path positions, reward kinds, timer durations, and curriculum version are retained. [Regression checks](../scripts/puzzles/check-progression.mjs) compare the identity manifest with the pre-edit baseline and exercise saved completions, access, coins, evidence, and active challenges. The findings below describe the original audit; the fraction and later expansion roadmap remains future work.

Pair-selection follow-up: all 19 `pair_sum` questions previously placed a valid pair in the first two positions and included ready-made alternative pairs. Both languages now retain the original target and intended arithmetic while supplying one valid pair and nearby distractors. Authored answer positions vary, and runtime shuffling covers both pair selection and pair hunts without changing the board during an attempt. Exhaustive pair checks verify distractors, remapped solution indices, duplicate-valued tiles, unchanged solutions, and catalog immutability across 64 shuffle seeds per puzzle.

The first three boards in each path include guidance; later boards use fresh operands and distinct results. The three intervening symbolic tasks remain as transfer practice. Controls are taps, with explicit exchange actions and a free board reset; drafts use the existing Check button, life costs and rewards. All IDs, skill identities, access and saved progress remain intact. Old completions are retained, not reclassified as evidence that a child has used the new board. The total remains 1,800 puzzles per language, now with 23 interaction types.

BrainPet should first repair difficulty jumps and predictable answer patterns, then deepen its treatment of place value, fractions, and decimals. Adding more examples from the current generators would increase the puzzle count without filling those gaps. The best next expansion is a connected elementary progression, with grade 6 topics as a later bridge. Covering grades 7 and 8 would require substantial new mathematical content and interactions.

The comparison uses all seven Duolingo grade guides, the generated English catalog, English/Latvian ID checks, puzzle renderers, authoring code, and progression checks. Individual tasks were sampled across the topics, with closer inspection of arithmetic, fractions, patterns, and measurement. Findings concern authored content and code, not observed child performance or a complete Latvian language review. Duolingo's guides describe its course; their grade labels are not a certification of alignment with Latvia's curriculum or any other national standard.

The [machine-readable counts](duolingo-math-audit-2026-11-01.json) distinguish the current path from the older curriculum structure.

At the audit baseline, BrainPet had 180 lessons and 1,800 puzzles per language, with 22 interaction types. Each current lesson contains four original practice tasks, three former boss tasks presented individually, one challenge, and two additional review tasks. New players finish all ten to unlock the next lesson in that topic. The challenge is therefore required on this path, although its timer is optional and defaults to off. Older earned access is preserved. See [current path construction and unlocking](../constants/puzzle-path.ts), [challenge mode](../app/play.tsx), and [path checks](../scripts/puzzles/check-paths.mjs).

Several earlier recommendations are already implemented. Introductory addition now stays with joining small groups, early fractions defer equivalence, comparison sides vary, plain numeric answer ranks are more balanced, and saved evidence distinguishes independent from supported success. Untimed challenges also exist. Retain these improvements. The older studies and curriculum document contain historical descriptions that should not override the current path code.

My design inference from these guides is to preserve a mathematical idea while changing how the child encounters it. For example, a quantity becomes a picture, a number-line position, and an equation. The child should then solve a fresh example with less instructional support. Merely changing the control or story does not establish that progression.

The task IDs above can be found in [Easy](../assets/puzzles/easy.json), [Medium](../assets/puzzles/medium.json), and [Hard](../assets/puzzles/hard.json). The underlying repetition comes from [task factories](../scripts/puzzles/build-content.mjs), [lesson expansion](../scripts/puzzles/expanded-lessons.mjs), and [research revisions](../scripts/puzzles/research-revisions.mjs). The first addition revision skips challenges, which explains why its improved teaching tasks still lead to the older, harder pair hunt.

Keep Easy, Medium, and Hard as navigation for now, but describe the skills each topic teaches. Their authored ages are 6 to 8, 8 to 10, and 10 to 12; those labels do not establish a grade mapping. A child may be ready for multiplication while needing introductory fraction work. For each lesson, specify the prerequisite, mathematical objective, number range, representation, reasoning demand, and common misconception. Increase one principal demand at the first encounter, then combine established skills in later applications.

For example, the first addition lesson could keep ten steps while using totals within ten throughout: join visible groups, connect a picture to a sum, solve fresh sums, choose the matching picture, complete a short story, and finish with independent variants. Move the pair hunt to a later lesson after children have learned to find missing parts. The task count need not increase to remove this jump.

The new puzzle families below would fill specific gaps. Their suggested placement describes a proposed progression, not official grade alignment.

Place-value, fraction-line, and fraction-strip tasks are the first implementation priorities. Add decimals after their prerequisites. Geometry then widens a catalog currently dominated by arithmetic. Negative numbers, ratios, equations, and coordinates form a later bridge. Probability, slopes, transformations, roots, and data analysis belong to a subsequent advanced curriculum with explicit prerequisites.

Difficulty should reflect the reasoning, not just the size of a numeral. Comparing 3/7 and 4/7 may be easier than comparing 1/3 and 1/4. A long operation route increases planning demands but does not teach linear equations. A function machine with one input/output pair and three candidate rules does not establish understanding of functions across a table and graph.

Use wrong answers to identify the next teaching action. For 27 + 16, an answer of 33 can prompt the child to inspect the unexchanged ten ones. For 1/3 versus 1/4, align equal-sized wholes. Preserve the attempted arrangement. Existing directional feedback for number lines, fractions, and routes is a useful base, but most choice tasks lack misconception-specific feedback. See [attempt hints](../utils/puzzle-attempt.ts).

Also distinguish immediate review from delayed retention. The two final path questions give more practice within the same session. They do not test whether the skill remains available days later. Preserve existing supported/independent evidence, but define review questions per skill and representation. The separate [review generator](../constants/learning-review.ts) currently reduces all pattern lessons to additive sequences and all measurement lessons to rectangle area. If that review route is used, those checks cannot establish retention of multiplicative patterns or unit rates. The current path's two added review questions are generated separately.

Implementation can proceed in three batches without expanding every topic at once.

1. Repair the current catalog. Align introductory challenges with their lessons, revise subtraction sequencing, balance truth values, repair estimation distractors, and vary fraction relationships. Make changes in authoring sources and regenerate both languages. Preserve earned progress and rewards; do not treat old completions as independent evidence of a newly introduced skill.
2. Pilot the first new interactions. Build place-value exchange and fractional number-line support, then fraction strips. Use complete short teaching sequences with fresh independent variants. Review the mathematics and Latvian wording before extending the generators. Number-line ticks currently assume unit steps, and the arithmetic helper rejects non-exact division, so rational arithmetic needs deliberate support rather than just new question text. See [number-line rendering](../components/puzzle/NumberLineTrack.tsx) and [arithmetic evaluation](../utils/puzzle-math.ts).
3. Expand by demonstrated need. Add decimal and geometry sequences, then the grade 6 bridge. Measure first-attempt success on unseen examples, support use, specific errors, and a later check. Observe whether children can explain or reproduce the relationship outside the familiar control. Completion totals alone cannot show that these changes improved learning.
